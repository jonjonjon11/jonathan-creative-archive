# 1. Vision and design values

## The request being preserved

Jonathan asked for a consolidated record of the best ideas and data across prior work: educational VR, music, education, simulation, martial arts, gyms, rescue, and medical ideas. The intended audience is broader than the original creator. The archive is meant to help altruistically motivated people and their AI tools discover useful ideas, understand unfinished attempts, and build on them. [SRC-001]

The request for “all” is treated as a desired direction, not as evidence that all sources are available. A prior synthesis itself reported partial recovery. This edition keeps missing material visible rather than filling it with invented history. [SRC-002]

## A shared direction

Across the reviewed projects, a coherent direction emerges: give people places to learn by exploring, to practice through play, to express themselves through movement and sound, and to cooperate in caring for one another. This paragraph is an editorial synthesis, not a quotation or a claim that every earlier project used the same design.

The educational universe offers a space in which a scientific model can be inspected and manipulated. Music offers another expressive and perceptual channel. A martial arts community adds embodied practice, mentorship, etiquette, and a shared place. Rescue-oriented games ask whether progress can mean restoring safety, knowledge, infrastructure, and relationships rather than merely accumulating weapons or surviving longer. [SRC-004, SRC-007, SRC-010, SRC-013]

These strands can inform one another without being forced into one product. A browser physics lesson, a cooperative rescue game, a local training club, and an unproven medicine-adjacent idea have different users, evidence standards, risks, and production needs.

## Principles worth carrying forward

1. **Make learning inspectable.** State the model, assumptions, units, uncertainty, and what a picture means.
2. **Make representations interchangeable.** The same underlying state may be shown visually, verbally, through sound, or through accessible controls. Changing a display should not silently change the model.
3. **Preserve the distinction between care and claims of efficacy.** A humane goal is valuable; it does not establish that a therapy, training system, or emergency tool works.
4. **Reward cooperation.** Consider progression that expands shared safety and capability.
5. **Design for participation.** Offer alternatives for sensory, mobility, skill, device, and social needs.
6. **Ship a small truthful result.** A working, tested learning example is more useful than a huge catalog whose status is unclear.
7. **Keep sources and rights attached.** Reuse is only reliable when provenance and license obligations remain visible.

Items 1, 2, and 4 are supported by recurring source themes. The full numbered list is a proposed editorial design charter for future builders, not an adopted governance policy.

## What this archive will not pretend to be

It is not a biography, a certified scientific encyclopedia, an operational medical platform, a finished metaverse, a validated business plan, or a complete backup. It does not portray incomplete projects as moral failures. Its job is to preserve useful reasoning and practical paths forward while saying clearly what is missing.
