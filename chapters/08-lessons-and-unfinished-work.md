# 8. What was tried, what remains, and what can be learned

## Do not invent a failure history

Jonathan explicitly asked to preserve work that was tried and not successfully made. That request does not authorize an archivist to diagnose every incomplete project as a failed implementation. Some sources show ideas, some documents, some code, and some historical tests. Where the cause of an unfinished state is unknown, this archive says so. [SRC-001]

## Evidence-supported unfinished states

### Reality Atlas

The reviewed default-branch status describes a limited set of scientific renderers and historical checks, while explicitly leaving several platform and interaction capabilities unverified or absent. The archive now includes the unchanged CC0 Appearance 0.3.1 standalone and 46 fresh focused calculation/syntax checks. Fresh browser testing was blocked before load; rendered interaction and exports remain unverified in this review. Additional branches extend the apparent scope. A future review must compare exact branches and commits rather than assuming the default branch is the latest or complete picture. [SRC-007, SRC-009]

**Reusable lesson:** publish a capability matrix with separate evidence for numerical tests, browser behavior, GPU paths, headset execution, audio, multiplayer, and engine integration.

### AR Buddy Commons

The project describes itself as pre-alpha. Its documentation and source establish a broad direction and concrete components, but orbital-flight source was not confirmed compiled or headset-tested by the reviewed evidence. [SRC-008]

**Reusable lesson:** a strong mission and extensive documentation are useful inputs, but each feature needs an executable demonstration and its own acceptance evidence.

### Math/Matter handoff

The recovered publication receipt identifies a concrete contribution, draft pull request, and runtime files while withholding claims of Unity acceptance or runtime verification. [SRC-005]

**Reusable lesson:** preserve handoff state explicitly. “Written,” “committed,” “opened as a draft PR,” “built,” and “accepted” are different milestones.

### Community and gym concepts

Planning documents and concept-package delivery records exist. No opening, acquisition, construction, operational validation, or tested business outcome is established. The virtual gym remained at feasibility discussion in the reviewed source. [SRC-010, SRC-011, SRC-012, SRC-013]

**Reusable lesson:** keep site, business, community, instructional, and software feasibility separate. A polished plan is a planning artifact.

### Cooperative rescue world

A detailed idea was captured, including restoration, safe community spaces, recovered knowledge, and animal rescue. No playable implementation is established by that source. [SRC-014]

**Reusable lesson:** preserve the intended player experience and values before adding mechanics or production claims.

### Cooperative authoring and health-research scaffolds

Recovered ZIP contents establish real prior work: a shared mission packet with offline authoring/reference code and a PHYSIO-MESH R&D scaffold. Neither establishes a complete game integration or functioning medical device. [SRC-020, SRC-021]

**Reusable lesson:** a schema, risk register, claims ledger, and testable reference model are valuable deliverables. Preserve them as such rather than overstating maturity.

### Native Unity release blocker

Humanity VR recorded passing scoped experience/combat assertions and a built/signature-checked Quest APK, but its Windows checkpoint remained blocked by a native shutdown crash. No headset tracking, comfort, or installation acceptance was established. [SRC-022]

**Reusable lesson:** passing feature tests does not cancel a failing process-level release criterion. Keep blockers visible alongside positive results.

### Broad scientific catalog

A recovered catalog contains useful topic coverage but also unreviewed scientific assertions and questionable classifications. It is not imported as validated data. [SRC-006]

**Reusable lesson:** a large catalog needs granular citations and review states. Apparent completeness is less valuable than traceable correctness.

## Open recovery gaps

- Full musical history and original audio artifacts.
- Full account history and all prior conversation exports.
- Runtime behavior of non-default branches.
- Exact rights for mixed third-party assets and inherited code.
- Scientific and clinical validation of recovered medical-adjacent research designs.
- Deployment, user-study, or community-operation outcomes.

These are gaps in this review. They are not claims that the material never existed.

## The durable contribution

Even before a large application exists, clear questions, good interfaces between model and representation, honest status records, cooperative design ideas, and well-sourced handoffs can be useful to another builder. This archive aims to preserve that value without hiding uncertainty or transferring avoidable risk.
