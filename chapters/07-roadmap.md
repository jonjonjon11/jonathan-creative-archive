# 7. A proposed build roadmap

This is a new editorial recommendation, not a historical commitment by Jonathan or a claim that a team has agreed to deliver it. There are no promised dates or budgets.

## Stage 0: make the archive safe and usable

**Output:** a curated documentation release with machine-readable concept and source records.

**Done when:** private information and credentials are excluded; rights are resolved; every historical claim has a source ID; private locator policy is decided; limitations are visible; all internal links and JSON records validate; the owner approves the exact release contents and public destination.

Do not publish existing repositories wholesale to accomplish this stage. Existing history and asset collections need their own separate review.

## Stage 1: reproduce one established model

**Output:** a desktop/browser lesson using a simple, clearly scoped mathematical model.

**Done when:** equations and assumptions are documented; expected behavior is tested; an independent source supports the lesson; numerical and display transformations are separately tested; a reproducible run can be exported; keyboard and textual access work; the build runs on a named tested environment.

A harmonic oscillator or other bounded elementary model is a reasonable candidate. Selection is a proposal, not a recovered product decision.

## Stage 2: add history and alternative representations

**Output:** pause/replay, a history view, a plot/text view, and optionally a documented audio mapping.

**Done when:** model time and presentation time are distinguished; saved histories preserve units and sampling metadata; representation changes do not alter the model; audio is optional and bounded; non-audio access is equivalent in learning content.

This is the smallest meaningful test of the Temporal Geometry separation.

## Stage 3: test one embodied or cooperative experience

Choose one direction instead of attempting all of them at once:

- A comfort-conscious XR version of the tested lesson.
- A virtual gym timing or movement sandbox explicitly presented as a game.
- A small cooperative restoration scenario.

**Done when:** the target device and user needs are explicit; the prototype is tested in that context; accessibility and consent decisions are documented; claimed results are limited to what was measured.

## Stage 4: expand through modular contributions

**Output:** additional lesson records, instruments, visual lenses, scenarios, or community activities.

**Done when:** contributors can add a small module without absorbing an entire engine project; every module carries sources, rights, assumptions, tests, and status; automated checks reject missing metadata and broken links.

Keep content and engine adapters separate. Allow a module to be valuable without the whole imagined universe existing.

## Stage 5: evaluate consequential deployments separately

A physical gym, a school deployment, a public social space, an emergency-training system, and a clinical tool each require separate governance and expertise. They should not be unlocked automatically by software progress.

Examples of decision gates include safeguarding, qualified instruction, local legal/code obligations, professional validation, privacy review, security, moderation, and operational accountability. These are categories for expert review, not a complete compliance checklist.

## Useful work that can start from this draft

- Verify one scientific claim against a suitable source.
- Inspect one non-default branch and record what runs.
- Identify one original asset and its license.
- Improve one lesson's text accessibility.
- Turn one unbounded idea into a falsifiable question or a small experiment.
- Recover one missing musical or design artifact with provenance.
- Compare one historical claim of completion with actual acceptance evidence.

Small reliable additions are the preferred unit of progress.
