# Cancer biology and evidence foundations

This lesson connects cell biology to the evidence needed to understand a cancer study. It is an original educational synthesis, stored with a [searchable evidence pack](../../data/health/cancer-foundations.json). The core text works offline. Source links provide a path back to the inspected material.

## From cells to diverse cancers

Cells normally regulate when they divide, repair damage, specialize, and die. Cancer develops when changes disrupt those controls. Some changes increase growth signals; others weaken growth restraints or DNA repair. A **driver** change helps cancer develop or persist. A change merely observed in a tumor is not automatically a driver: it may have little effect, an unknown effect, or an effect that depends on its biological context. [NCI: What Is Cancer?](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer)

Cancer is therefore a family of diseases. Cell lineage, location, extent of spread, and molecular features all contribute information. Many cancers form solid tumors; blood cancers need not form a solid mass. **Metastasis** means that cells from an original cancer establish cancer elsewhere. The destination does not erase the original identity.

“Genetic” also differs from “inherited.” Cancer-related changes can arise during life through errors in cell division or environmental damage, as well as through inherited susceptibility. An inherited risk variant is not a prediction that every carrier will develop cancer. Cells within one tumor can differ, and their mixture can change over time. Those differences help explain why a single mechanism or sample does not describe every cancer cell. [NCI: What Is Cancer?](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer)

## Read molecular information in context

A **biomarker** is a measurable biological feature used for a specified purpose. In cancer, tests may examine DNA changes, proteins, or other features. Tumor profiling asks about features of the cancer; germline testing asks about inherited variants. A tumor result can suggest an inherited finding, but confirmation may require a different test and interpretation. It does not establish relatives’ genetic status. [NCI: Biomarker Testing](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/precision-medicine/tumor-dna-sequencing)

Keep the question attached to the measurement. A **prognostic** use concerns likely disease course or outcome. A **predictive** use concerns response to a particular intervention. An association with worse outcomes alone does not show which intervention changes those outcomes. The same marker may serve different roles in different settings. [NCI: Tumor Markers](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet); [FDA: Biomarker Categories](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/biomarker-qualification-program/about-biomarkers-and-qualification)

A matched biomarker is a reason to investigate relevant evidence, not a guarantee of response. Other cell populations, biological pathways, and changes over time can matter. Record the exact alteration, specimen, test method, sampling time, cancer context, and evidence supporting its proposed use.

## Ask what a study actually measures

Clinical trials develop evidence in stages. Early phases emphasize safety and initial activity; later comparisons examine how an intervention performs against a relevant standard. Eligibility determines who is studied. Randomized assignment determines how participants enter comparison groups. These are different functions. [NCI: Trial Phases](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/clinical-trials/what-are-trials/phases)

Endpoints also answer different questions:

- **Objective response** concerns tumor shrinkage that meets predefined criteria, often with a required duration.
- **Progression-free survival (PFS)** concerns time until progression or death, using the study’s definitions and analysis rules.
- **Overall survival (OS)** concerns time until death from any cause.

A study can show a change in one endpoint without establishing a change in another. Record the endpoint definition, starting time, follow-up, comparison, missing observations, and uncertainty. Response duration and assessment schedules matter alongside response counts. [FDA: Clinical Trial Endpoints, sections III.A, III.B.2, III.B.4](https://www.fda.gov/media/71195/download)

## Three worked examples

### 1. Identify the cancer, not just the location

A hypothetical record says “breast cancer metastatic to lung.” The origin is breast cancer; the lung is a metastatic site. Calling it primary lung cancer would merge two different classifications. Preserve both the original cancer and the spread location.

### 2. Separate an observation from an inherited finding

A hypothetical tumor report identifies variant X. First record the specimen as tumor tissue. Next ask whether inherited status was independently assessed. The report alone cannot establish that the variant is inherited, that relatives carry it, or that it predicts response. Each conclusion requires its own evidence.

### 3. Calculate a response difference without inventing survival

These are invented teaching data: 20 of 100 participants meet a predefined response criterion in group A; 12 of 100 do so in group B. The observed proportions are 20% and 12%, an **8 percentage-point** difference. These counts alone do not give response duration, PFS, OS, or the uncertainty of a treatment-effect analysis. Preserve the response calculation and request the other outcome data separately.

## Practice, then check

1. A tumor contains several genetic variants. Must each be a driver?
2. A cancer spreads from the colon to the liver. Does location alone make it primary liver cancer?
3. A marker is associated with poor outcomes in an untreated cohort. Has a treatment benefit been demonstrated?
4. In invented teaching data, 18 of 60 participants meet a response criterion. What proportion responded? Does it determine OS?
5. What information would let another researcher interpret a reported PFS difference?

**Answers:**

1. No. Distinguish drivers, incidental changes, and variants of uncertain significance using additional evidence.
2. No. Preserve the colon origin and liver metastatic site; a separate primary cancer is a different possibility requiring evidence.
3. No. This describes prognostic information in that context. A claim about treatment response needs evidence concerning an intervention and a suitable comparison.
4. 18 / 60 = 0.30, or 30%. OS requires time-to-death data and its analysis; it cannot be recovered from response counts.
5. Record the population, comparator, endpoint definition, time origin, assessment schedule, follow-up, censoring and missing-data rules, estimate, uncertainty, and analysis plan.
